About

I chose medical devices because they force engineering discipline: the design must be manufacturable, safe, and reliable in unpredictable clinical environments. That challenge is exactly what motivates me.

My B.S. in Biomedical Engineering (June 2025) built the clinical and biological foundation. My incoming M.S. in Mechanical Engineering (Biomechanics & Biomedical Engineering) deepens my focus on mechanics, device architecture, and system-level performance.

I approach projects as an R&D engineer: define requirements, identify failure modes early, prototype quickly, and validate with structured test methods. Across orthopedic tools, robotics-based test automation, and machine-vision inspection systems, I focus on reducing uncertainty before design freeze.
