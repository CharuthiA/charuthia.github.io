Case Study: TPLO Orthopedic Drill Bit

Problem

Existing drill tools showed variable cutting efficiency and coating durability under repeated use, creating risk to surgical precision and instrument lifespan.

Design Approach

I developed a stainless-steel drill bit geometry focused on chip evacuation, edge stability, and manufacturable tolerances. CAD iterations prioritized flute profile and tip geometry that balanced aggressiveness with control.

Engineering Decisions

  • Material: stainless steel for corrosion resistance and sterilization compatibility.
  • Tolerance strategy: GD&T controls on critical concentricity and runout features.
  • Risk controls: identified likely failure modes (edge wear, coating delamination, heat buildup) and mapped each to a verification method.

Testing & Validation

I built a verification plan around requirements-level metrics rather than anecdotal feel.

  • Torque characterization: measured peak and steady-state torque through surrogate cortical material at controlled feed rates to verify cutting-load targets.
  • Axial force testing: captured insertion force profiles to evaluate stability and detect chatter onset conditions.
  • Wear testing: ran repeated drilling cycles and monitored geometry drift, edge rounding, and performance decay.
  • Coating degradation: inspected pre/post-cycle surfaces and correlated coating loss regions with localized stress/contact behavior.
  • Repeatability: compared results across operators and fixtures to separate tool behavior from test-setup variability.

Tools & Methods

SolidWorks, GD&T drawings, benchtop torque/force instrumentation, controlled fixture setup, and Python scripts for post-test trend analysis.

Results & Tradeoffs

The design met functional cutting targets and delivered more stable force signatures across repeated use. The main tradeoff was between higher initial aggressiveness and longer-term edge retention, which informed the final geometry and coating selection window.
