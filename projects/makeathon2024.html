Case Study: Automated Test + Vision Inspection Systems

Problem

Manual testing and label inspection introduced operator variability, inconsistent data capture, and slower throughput in development and manufacturing support workflows.

Design Approach

I architected a modular automation stack: UR5e-driven test motions, sensor-based force capture, and machine-vision checks (OCR + label verification) for quality gates.

Engineering Decisions

  • Separated motion control, acquisition, and analysis layers to simplify debugging and future validation.
  • Used deterministic robot paths and fixture constraints to minimize setup degrees of freedom.
  • Implemented threshold-based pass/fail logic with traceable raw data retention for investigations.

Testing & Validation

  • System repeatability: performed repeated runs on fixed samples to establish baseline variance for force metrics and OCR confidence scores.
  • Gage-style assessment: compared automated vs. manual results to quantify operator-effect reduction.
  • Fault injection: introduced intentional label offsets and contrast changes to characterize false accept/reject behavior.
  • Robustness checks: tested across lighting and alignment perturbations to define operational limits and setup requirements.
  • Data integrity validation: verified synchronized timestamps, sample IDs, and test metadata to preserve traceability.

Tools & Methods

UR5e robotics, Python, sensor interfaces, OpenCV, Cognex/Keyence vision workflows, and statistical analysis scripts.

Results & Tradeoffs

The platform reduced variability and increased consistency of both test execution and inspection outcomes. Tradeoffs included upfront fixture engineering effort and tighter calibration requirements to maintain reliable OCR performance.
